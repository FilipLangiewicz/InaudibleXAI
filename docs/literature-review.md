# Literature review

This file describes the papers that the T1 work uses. T1 has three parts:

- the masking threshold (model 1),
- the sub-threshold deletion,
- the first measurement on AST and ESC-50 with a listening test.

The review has only the papers that the team read in full. The numbering is the numbering of `docs/design_proposal.md`. Dompteur is a new paper. The team found it in T1.

Status: 2026-11-06 (end of T1). "Not given" means that the paper does not report the value.

Add a paper to this file when a task uses it. The other papers of `docs/design_proposal.md` come into this file at T2 to T6.

| 1 | L. Schönherr, K. Kohls, S. Zeiler, T. Holz, D. Kolossa. *Adversarial Attacks Against Automatic Speech Recognition Systems via Psychoacoustic Hiding.* NDSS 2019. [arXiv:1808.05665](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05665) | A targeted attack on Kaldi (DNN-HMM, WSJ recipe). The hearing thresholds come from the psychoacoustic model of MP3. The thresholds scale the gradient, and a margin λ (dB) lets the perturbation go above them. | No code repository. A demo page has audio examples. The model is the public Kaldi WSJ recipe. | WER to the target text. φ: the mean excess of the perturbation over the threshold (dB). SNR. A transcription test (22 listeners) and a MUSHRA test (30 listeners, 3 excluded). | One 6-core Intel i7-4960X CPU, no GPU. Less than 2 min for a 10 s clip (500 iterations). | The attack adds energy below the threshold. Our deletion removes energy below the threshold. The two use the same type of MPEG threshold. The authors normalize the largest bin to 95 dB. Model 1 uses 96 dB. With λ = 20 dB, φ falls from 10.11 dB (no threshold) to 5.49 dB. Thus the threshold does not remove all audible change. The listeners understood the original text equally well (WER 12.59 % and 12.61 %). An MP3 encoding did not give back the original text, and it also damaged the target text. | |
| 2 | Y. Qin, N. Carlini, I. Goodfellow, G. Cottrell, C. Raffel. *Imperceptible, Robust, and Targeted Adversarial Examples for Automatic Speech Recognition.* ICML 2019. [arXiv:1903.10346](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.10346) | A targeted attack on Lingvo (sequence-to-sequence) with LibriSpeech. A hinge loss keeps the perturbation PSD below the masking threshold of the clean audio. A second attack also works in simulated rooms. | No code in the paper. A project page has audio examples. The model is Lingvo. | Success rate and WER. A study with 80 Amazon Mechanical Turk users (3 excluded) and 3 tests: "clean or noisy", "identify the original", "identical or not". | GPU not given. Adam, 1000 iterations (stage 1) and 4000 iterations (stage 2). | The threshold follows Lin and Abdulla (2015). It treats all maskers as tonal, has a two-slope spreading function and has no decimation. The STFT has 2048 samples and a hop of 512. The PSD is normalized to 96 dB, as in model 1. This threshold is a candidate for model 2 (T2) because it is simpler than model 1. The listeners called the attack "identical" to the original in 76 % of the pairs. For two copies of the same clip, the value was 85 %. Thus a direct comparison finds part of a sub-threshold change. This result supports a forced-choice test (ABX) for our deletion. | |
| 3 | D. A. Kelly, H. Chockler. *I Guess That's Why They Call it the Blues: Causal Analysis for Audio Classifiers.* ACM MM 2026. [arXiv:2601.16675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16675) | FreqReX: a black-box causal method. It finds sufficient, necessary and complete subsets of the Fourier frequencies of a clip. Then it changes these frequencies to change the class. | The paper puts the code into the supplementary material. The ReX tool is public ([ReX-XAI/ReX](https://github.com/ReX-XAI/ReX)). The models are public fine-tuned models from Hugging Face. | The share of frequencies that is necessary for sufficiency. The success rate of the attack with 1 and 5 frequencies. The success rate of the STFT attack. | NVIDIA A40 GPUs. | Five models on GTZAN (AST, Wav2Vec2, two DistilHuBERT, Whisper) and three on RAVDESS. A change to 1 of approximately 240,000 frequencies changes the class in 58 % of the clips. With 5 frequencies, the value is 78 %. The STFT attack gives 62 %. AST is the most resistant model (success 0.48). The paper calls the change "practically inaudible" but has no masking model and no listening test. Our project gives this claim a definition with the masking threshold. The changes often do not survive the conversion to integer WAV. Thus we must state if a model gets float data or 16-bit WAV. | |
| 9 | P. Kitłowski, D. Wiącek, M. Modrzejewski. *The Perceived Fragility of Explanations in Audio Models: Manipulation of Attribution with Unchanged Predictions.* ICML 2026. [arXiv:2606.14466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14466) | An attack moves the Grad-CAM and LRP maps of deepfake music detectors and keeps the predicted class. A loss term penalizes the perturbation energy above a static masking threshold of the clean input. | [cncPomper/Audio-XAI](https://github.com/cncPomper/Audio-XAI). The models are public: VGGish, AST, SpecTTTra-γ-5s. | Cosine similarity and Top-10 overlap of the maps. Audio Fragility Score. PESQ, STOI, ViSQOL, PEAQ, Zimtohrli, CDPAM. | PLGrid (ACK Cyfronet AGH). The hardware is not given. | 100 recordings from SONICS. The paper does not name the masking model. For AST, the psychoacoustic attack gives a median ViSQOL of 4.64 and a median PEAQ of −2.00. AST is the most fragile model (mean rank 3.00). SpecTTTra is the most resistant (mean rank 7.83). Clips with much broadband energy are easier to attack, because they give a larger masking budget. Our T1 band check shows the same effect: the deletion removes background noise in wide bands. The paper has no listening test, no control and no intervals. Our project measures the map mass below the threshold without an attack. | |
| new | T. Eisenhofer, L. Schönherr, J. Frank, L. Speckemeier, D. Kolossa, T. Holz. *Dompteur: Taming Audio Adversarial Examples.* 2021. [arXiv:2102.05431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05431) | A defence for Kaldi. It removes all STFT bins below the MPEG-1 hearing thresholds plus a margin Φ (dB), and it adds a band-pass filter (200 to 7000 Hz). Then it trains Kaldi again on the filtered data. | [rub-syssec/dompteur](https://github.com/rub-syssec/dompteur): code, pretrained models and audio examples. | WER on WSJ eval92 (333 utterances). Segmental SNR. A MUSHRA test with 355 Amazon Mechanical Turk users. | Intel Xeon Gold 6130, 128 GB RAM, four NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti. | The mask is the same rule as our deletion: a bin goes to zero if its level is below the threshold plus Φ. Our deletion uses Φ = 0. The unmodified Kaldi gets a WER of 5.90 % on the original input and 8.74 % on the filtered input. Thus a public model uses energy below the threshold. This is the first measurement of our question, for one ASR model and without a control between clips. The paper must cite it. A model that the authors trained on filtered data changes little (6.50 % to 6.20 %). A sweep over Φ is a possible check of the sensitivity of our result. | |

## Conclusions for the next steps

1. **Model 2 (T2).** The threshold of Qin et al. (Lin and Abdulla 2015) is well documented and simpler than model 1. It is a good second masking model.
2. **Listening test (T2).** Schönherr et al. and Qin et al. show that listeners find part of a sub-threshold change in a direct comparison. Use an ABX test with more trials.
3. **Objective measures (T2).** Kitłowski et al. use PEAQ and ViSQOL. Add them next to the per-bin check and the band check.
4. **Input format (T3).** Kelly and Chockler show that small changes do not survive the conversion to 16-bit WAV. Give the models float data and record this in the config.
5. **Paper.** Dompteur measured the effect of the deletion on Kaldi (WER from 5.90 % to 8.74 %). Our paper must cite it and show what is new: 15 model and task pairs, a control between clips, intervals and the attribution maps.
